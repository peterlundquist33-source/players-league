Chase Against the Field, Literally

Isaac's whole season-opening pitch is Ja'Marr Chase plus depth nobody else has, and it's a legitimate argument. Malik Nabers and Terry McLaurin as your WR3-adjacent options is absurd, and even with Chase soaking up targets this group projects six points clear of Kaleb's receivers. The problem is everywhere else. Javonte Williams and Cam Skattebo are touchdown-dependent committee backs, Bo Nix is a fine but capped floor at QB, and Hockenson-or-Kincaid at tight end is a rounding error either way.

Ashton Jeanty and Breece Hall give him the best backfield in the matchup by seven points on paper, Justin Herbert is the best quarterback either team runs out, and Harold Fannin gives him a real tight end instead of a coin flip. Drake London and Ladd McConkey are plenty behind Chase's group without being an actual weakness. The projections have Kaleb up two, which feels about right — Isaac needs Chase to go nuclear and one of his shaky backs to hit a score just to survive the floor Kaleb's roster provides everywhere else.

Hurts and Barkley Give Rezac the Edge

Adam opens his title defense against a Logan roster built like a mini Eagles reunion, and that's the whole problem. Jalen Hurts projects for 21, Saquon Barkley another 18, and that duo alone outproduces Adam's best guys at both spots combined. Adam counters with Jayden Daniels and a Kenneth Walker/Bucky Irving committee that's fine but unspectacular, the kind of RB room that needs garbage-time work to hit its number. Where Adam claws back is the perimeter — Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Chris Olave give him two legit spike weeks, and Sam LaPorta at tight end is a quiet two-point edge over Isaiah Likely.

It's close on paper, about eight points by best-lineup math, but Logan's floor is just higher. Jeremiyah Love gives him a second bell-cow behind Barkley, and Justin Jefferson anchors a receiver room deep enough that Tetairoa McMillan and Michael Pittman are afterthoughts rather than needs. Adam's margin for error is thin: if either QB or a receiver underperforms, there's not much juice behind Rome Odunze and Jalen Coker to bail him out. Logan should win this one running away from the RB and QB spots alone.

Gibbs and Depth Have Mitchell's Number

Leif opens the season with the flashier top of the roster and none of the connective tissue. Josh Allen gives him a real ceiling at QB, and McCaffrey-Henry is a two-headed monster if both stay healthy, which is doing a lot of work in that sentence. But look past the names and it thins out fast — Woody Marks and Kaelon Black as your RB depth, Caleb Douglas as your fifth receiver, Jason Myers as a top-10 asset on this roster. Marvin Harrison Jr. and Jayden Reed need to actually hit rookie-year and sophomore-leap upside for this to work, not just flash it.

Mitchell's roster doesn't have a marquee name at the top so much as it has quality at every single slot. Jahmyr Gibbs is a true bell cow with receiving work baked in, Nico Collins is a legit WR1, and Tyler Warren gives him a real receiving tight end instead of touchdown-or-bust guesswork. Even his QB2, Daniel Jones, would start on half the teams in this league. The projections have this close, but Mitchell's floor is a lot higher than Leif's, and in Week 1, with nobody's lineup locked and injuries lurking everywhere, floor wins.
