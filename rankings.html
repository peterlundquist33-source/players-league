2026 DRAFT GRADES

Roster quality + draft-value efficiency vs a 5-source consensus (ESPN ADP, ESPN, FantasyPros ECR, FantasyCalc, Sleeper) · curved · 2026-08-29

Nine draft rooms out of twelve would've told you Adam and Peter can't both nail a class in the same August, and yet here we are, the two most decorated names in this league running away with it again while the rest of you argued about handcuffs. John rides shotgun with them, which means the Lundquist bloodline is 2-for-3 on this page and John will absolutely mention that at Thanksgiving. The real story is the bottom of the barrel: Grant and Kaleb somehow drafted like they were trying to guarantee themselves lake-cabin golf attire, and Mitchell and Isaac weren't far behind, which means the dress race is going to be ugly and personal by Week 6. Everyone in the middle just showed up, took what the board gave them, and hoped nobody remembers this page in December.

Best value: Jayden Daniels — round 7, pick 79 (+38 vs market)
The Curse-Breaker Does It Again

The receiver room here is absurd. Jaxon Smith-Njigba at 7, Chris Olave falling to round 3, Rome Odunze as a WR5 — that's not a wideout corps, that's a monopoly. Sam LaPorta gives him a plug-and-play TE1 and then Adam just starts stealing. Jayden Daniels in round 7 is the best value pick in the entire league, a top-five quarterback drafted like a streamer, and he doubled down with Brock Purdy five rounds later as pure profit insurance. The running back room is the one soft spot — Kenneth Walker and Bucky Irving are fine, not great, and Dowdle/Gainwell/Robinson is a committee of question marks — but nobody builds a Ferrari and complains about the trunk space.

This is what efficient greed looks like. Adam already has the best career record in the league and no losing seasons, and this draft reads like a guy who refuses to have a bad year even by accident. The reigning champ just armed himself for another one.

Peter Remembers How To Draft

Bijan Robinson at pick 2 is as clean a foundation as this league saw all weekend, and Peter didn't stop building there. A.J. Lamar Jackson dropping to round 5 is the kind of gift you don't say no to, and Peter didn't. Tight end is a wasteland — Tucker Kraft alone against the world — but with this much thunder at receiver and a real QB1, it barely matters.

Yes, he overpaid late for Dak Prescott insurance and punted the tight end room entirely, but that's the footnote, not the headline. This is Peter's best draft since the 13-1 season that still defines him, and everyone in the group chat knows it. Younger Lundquist brother, older Lundquist bragging rights — this is how you start closing that gap.

Pukachu Builds a Two-Man Wrecking Crew

John's first two picks are the whole thesis: Puka Nacua at 1.4 for market price, then Brock Bowers at 2.21 and Trey McBride at 3.28, the last one a nine-spot steal that turns TE into a room grade of A+ almost by itself. The RB room behind Kyren is closer to theory than practice, and Brian Thomas Jr. at 69 overall cost him real draft capital for a room that still grades out F, but nobody builds a title around Blake Corum anyway.

This is Pukachu behaving exactly like a team that went 10-4 and still has no ring to show for it — refusing to punt any premium position, stacking two elite tight ends like insurance policies. Peter's still up 6-3 head to head, but John's roster says that gap is closing fast.

Best value: Michael Wilson — round 9, pick 104 (+25 vs market)

Backfield Loaded, Receivers an Afterthought

Noah built this draft around one obvious thesis: hoard the backfield, worry about the rest later. James Cook III at 8 and De'Von Achane at 17 is as good a RB1-RB2 as anyone in this league drafted, and Achane at plus-8 value is the single best pick on the board through two rounds. Drake Maye a full round early looks aggressive until you remember this is the same guy who backed into a title from the 7 seed — he doesn't panic about optics, he just takes his guy. Rhamondre and Tyjae Spears add real thump behind the studs.

The receiver room is where it falls apart. Garrett Wilson and Mike Evans are fine, but an F-grade WR corps means everything after that is band-aids — Josh Downs at 89 was a full round too early, and Romeo Doubs plus Michael Wilson feels like hoping the waiver wire fixes itself. Colston Loveland gives Maye a real weapon at least. Championship upside is real, provided receivers don't matter in the fantasy postseason again.

The Aura Farm Built a Wide Receiver Cult

Christian did what Christian does: hit the receiver room hard and let everything else sort itself out. CeeDee Lamb at 11 is the safest floor pick in the draft, and stacking DeVonta Smith, Jameson Williams, and a stolen Khalil Shakir at pick 131 gives him more weekly firepower on the outside than almost anyone in the league. The RB room is fine, not scary — Chase Brown and Travis Etienne share touches with other people, and Dylan Sampson is a lottery ticket, not a plan. George Kittle at 86 is the steal of his draft, borderline theft.

The QB room is where the aura runs out. Trevor Lawrence and Jordan Love is a bye-week disaster waiting to happen, and reaching for Deebo 17 spots early didn't help. Fitting, honestly — this is a 10-4-then-4-10 build if there ever was one, all ceiling, no floor, never the last man standing but never boring either.

Solid Bones, No Curse-Breaking Magic Yet

Logan built this the sensible way: Justin Jefferson at ten, Saquon in round two, then he stole Jeremiyah Love twelve slots under market in round three. The receiver room is legitimately deep — Jefferson, Tetairoa McMillan, Michael Pittman, Luther Burden — and grabbing Jalen Hurts as a QB2-priced QB1 in round six is the kind of theft that actually moves a draft grade. Saquon and Dobbins give him real bell-cow equity even with Love in the mix.

The soft spot is obvious: tight end is a wasteland, Isaiah Likely isn't fixing that, and the QB room outside Hurts is empty air. Mike Washington in round thirteen was a reach nobody needed to make. Solid, unspectacular, efficient in the middle rounds — which, fittingly, is exactly the profile of a guy who's taken the one seed twice and has nothing to show for it. Same story, better receivers.

Leif built a monster in the middle rounds and forgot the receiver room existed. Christian McCaffrey and Derrick Henry give him arguably the scariest one-two punch at running back in the league, and grabbing Josh Allen at pick 29 after Patrick Mahomes fell into his lap in round 13 means this roster has two MVP-caliber quarterbacks and nobody to throw the ball to. Davante Adams, Marvin Harrison Jr. and Stefon Diggs is a serviceable trio on paper, but it's held together by name recognition and prayer at this point in their careers, and the WR room grades out as the worst in the league.

The efficiency numbers back up the eye test — reaching for Woody Marks and Kaelon Black cost him real draft capital for handcuffs nobody needed. This is Joey Lunchbox in a nutshell: two elite positions, one disaster area, and somehow still finding a way to be the guy who almost had it figured out. Loyal to the bit, 22-34 and counting.

Gibbs Carries A Draft That Forgot Receivers

Mitchell nailed the top of this thing. Jahmyr Gibbs at 1.1 is the right pick regardless of format, Josh Jacobs in round three gives him a legitimate two-back attack, and Tyler Warren plus a shockingly well-timed Travis Kelce flier in round ten makes tight end an actual strength. Then the wheels come off. DJ Moore, Courtland Sutton, Matthew Golden, Tank Dell, Jerry Jeudy — that's a receiver room built entirely out of "well, somebody has to play WR3," and none of them were values, they were just guys who happened to still be there. Jaxson Dart and Daniel Jones at quarterback isn't a plan, it's a hostage situation.

The efficiency number tells the real story: outside of Kelce and Alvin Kamara falling into his lap late, Mitchell paid retail or worse most of the night, capped by reaching for Rachaad White fourteen picks early. Fitting for the human coin flip — flip enough times, land on 7-7 again.

Best value: Cam Skattebo — round 5, pick 51 (+10 vs market)

Six Wideouts Deep, Zero Running Backs Wide

Isaac built a wide receiver room that could win the league by itself — Chase, Nabers, McLaurin, Metcalf, Wan'Dale, Meyers is genuinely absurd depth, and taking Chase third overall is the one pick nobody can argue with. Problem is that's the whole draft. He punted the backfield, reaching for Javonte Williams in round two like it was a scarcity year, then didn't touch another back until Skattebo and Warren on Day 3 fill-ins. Bo Nix forty spots early in round ten and Kincaid thirty-one over market in round twelve are the kind of panic-fills that happen when you realize in round nine you drafted six wideouts and no plan.

This is a team that wins shootouts in November and loses on bye weeks. Isaac's whole existence since 2022 has been chasing that 11-3 start, and stacking WRs like lottery tickets instead of building a roster is exactly the kind of romantic, self-destructive move that's kept him from getting back there.

A Wide Receiver Museum, No Foundation

Grant built a WR room that would win most leagues by itself — Amon-Ra St. Brown at pick 6, Zay Flowers falling to round 3, plus Waddle, Watson, and Godwin stacked on top. That is a genuinely good collection of catches. Problem is a fantasy team needs a quarterback and a running back too, and Grant seems to have remembered this around round 10, when he shrugged and took Kyler Murray next to Baker Mayfield, then closed with Tank Bigsby as his RB security blanket. The backfield behind Omarion Hampton is Jadarian Price and Jacory Croskey-Merritt, drafted a full two rounds ahead of where anyone else had them.

The QB room is a disaster and the RB room isn't much better, and reaching hard for bench pieces at positions of need doesn't count as addressing them. Classic Grant — allergic to balance, allergic to plans, three wins or nine wins, nobody knows.

The Cinderella Sends Regrets This Time

Picking dead last overall says plenty on its own, but the tape confirms it. Ashton Jeanty at 12 was already the softest possible start to a rebuild year, and it snowballed — Jordan Addison in round seven at 24 points under market is the kind of reach that gets flagged at the cabin before the next pick is even in. Keenan Allen and KC Concepcion cost more than the room could afford to pay, and the result is a receiver corps that grades D+ next to Drake London and not much else worth trusting. Kaleb broke through as the original worst-to-first story in 2023, but three straight 8-6 seasons since suggest the magic isn't walking through that cabin door on its own. This draft doesn't help.
