Roster quality + draft-value efficiency vs a 5-source consensus (ESPN ADP, ESPN, FantasyPros ECR, FantasyCalc, Sleeper) · curved · 2026-08-29

Three A's at the top and the draft still had a clean split down the middle — Adam, Peter, and John all walked out of the cabin with rosters that look finished, while the bottom five (Mitchell, Isaac, CJ, Grant, Kaleb) are already praying for a waiver-wire miracle by Week 4. Running backs went in bunches early and often, which is exactly why Noah's zoo of a roster and Logan's ground-and-pound build both grade out fine despite completely different strategies. Christian and Leif did the sensible middle-class thing and nobody will remember their teams until October. Grant and Kaleb's F's are the kind you frame and hang on the wall — a real achievement in the wrong direction.

WR Room So Deep It's Rude

Adam's actually done building rosters, he's just showing off now. Jaxon Smith-Njigba anchors a wide receiver corps that goes four deep before you hit a bad word — Chris Olave at pick 31 is theft, Rome Odunze gives him a legit WR3, and Jalen Coker in round 10 is pure lottery-ticket depth. Sam LaPorta anchors a shaky TE room that Mark Andrews (round 11, absurd value) quietly upgrades into respectable. Efficiency plus ceiling is how you get a 228.1 roster score and a +50 value score in the same draft. This is what happens when the guy with the best career record in the league stops overpaying out of habit — 34-22 doesn't happen by accident, and neither does this.

Logan Builds A Roster, Not A Curse-Breaker

Justin Jefferson at 10 and Saquon Barkley at 15 is about as clean a start as this league saw, and Logan kept pulling value after — Jeremiyah Love in Round 3 as a legitimate handcuff-to-lead-back bet, Tetairoa McMillan as a real WR2 in Carolina's offense, and Jalen Hurts falling to Round 6 is the single best value grab of the entire draft. Michael Pittman and Xavier Worthy add cheap volume, and Isaiah Likely was stolen a round late. The floor here is genuinely high.

The cracks: tight end is a wasteland behind Likely's injury risk, and the RB room outside Barkley and Love is Dobbins' knees and Tyler Allgeier's touches on a run-funnel offense. That's the profile of a team that wins the regular season and finds a new way to lose in January — which, given Logan's the reigning face of the #1 Seed Curse and the dress, tracks perfectly.
