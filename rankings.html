2026 DRAFT GRADES

Absolute 0–100 score (not curved) — roster quality vs fixed positional benchmarks on a 5-source consensus (ESPN, ESPN ADP, FantasyPros ECR, FantasyCalc, Sleeper), plus a draft-value adjustment · 2026-08-29

Draft season shook out top-heavy this year — Adam somehow finds another gear after already owning the best resume in league history, and the tier right behind him (Peter, Christian, Noah) all left the cabin with rosters that look like they printed money. The middle class is crowded and unspectacular, which usually means somebody's B- team quietly wins the thing while everybody argues about tiebreakers. Running back went in bursts all night, with three different owners reaching in the same six-pick window trying to avoid the cliff everyone saw coming. Bottom third is where it gets grim — Isaac, Leif, and Grant all landed in the same gentleman's-C purgatory, which at Players Weekend just means somebody's picking out a dress in December.

Loaded at the skill spots, thin at running back

Adam built this the way he builds everything — steady, top-heavy where it counts, and light on panic. Jaxon Smith-Njigba at 7 was already good value, and Chris Olave falling to round 3 gave him a top-ten receiver on top of it. Rome Odunze in round 5 is a bet on name recognition more than current cost, but the WR room carries this team either way. The real coup came in round 7, where Jayden Daniels sitting at pick 79 is the single best value anybody drafted this year — a QB5 talent for QB-nobody-cared price, backed up by Brock Purdy a round later at another absurd discount. Sam LaPorta in round 6 is the one swing that missed, a full round early for a TE8.

Running back is the soft spot. Kenneth Walker was a reach at 18, Bucky Irving and Brian Robinson cost more than late-RB rooms usually justify, and depth behind them is Rico Dowdle and Kenny Gainwell — capable, unspectacular, one injury from ugly. Still, the top of this roster is a real problem for the league, and Adam knows it. He's 34-22 for a reason. He just needs Isaac to stop being the one guy who has his number.

Still trailing John 3-6, still chasing 2022. This is the closest he's come back.

Jameson Williams and Khalil Shakir add ceiling depth behind them. The problem is everything drafted at running back before the double-digit rounds: Chase Brown and Travis Etienne both cost more than their current standing supports, Bhayshul Tuten and Aaron Jones are complementary pieces being asked to be answers. George Kittle at TE was an overpay, and Deebo Samuel in round 9 is Christian's worst pick by a mile, a 56th-ranked receiver taken like a top-40 one. The saving grace is the quarterback room — Trevor Lawrence as a value pickup, then Jordan Love falling to round 14 was his best pick of the draft, pure profit at the position.

Christian's whole deal is boom-or-bust, 10-4 one year and 4-10 the next, and this roster reads exactly like that: an elite top end at receiver carrying a shaky running back corps that could sink him again.

Logan built this the only way that makes sense in a league where he's cursed at the top of the standings: pay for stars and worry about depth later. Justin Jefferson anchors the receiving corps, and pairing him with Jalen Hurts — a sixth-round QB who plays for the team Saquon Barkley now runs behind — gives this roster real top-end thump. Barkley came at pick 15, pricier than his current RB8 slot, but Jeremiyah Love at 34 was his best value, a third-round flier on a back who profiles as a bigger name than his RB15 cost. Tetairoa McMillan and Luther Burden add rookie upside behind Jefferson, though both went a round early.

The cracks show late. Isaiah Likely at TE was his worst pick, a ninth-round reach for a room ESPN grades a D outright, and the RB and WR benches thin out fast into replacement-level names like Dobbins and Allgeier. Still, A-grade QB and WR rooms carry this. Logan's owned back-to-back top seeds and zero playoff wins — the stars are here again, the depth is the same old excuse waiting to happen.
