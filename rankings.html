2026 DRAFT GRADES

Every pick vs a 4-source consensus (ESPN ADP, ESPN, FantasyCalc, Sleeper) · graded on the curve · generated 2026-08-29

Another Players Weekend, another draft that Adam Stockwell treated like a formality — Mr. Consistent walks away with the A+ again, because of course he does, the man is allergic to losing seasons and apparently also to bad boards. Christian wasn't far behind, but everyone else in the middle tier basically drafted to a shrug: solid-not-special from Logan, CJ and Noah, the kind of rosters that win you nothing at Players Weekend and nothing in December either. The real story is the bottom of the class — Leif, Peter, Kaleb and Grant all turned in drafts bad enough that someone in that group is measuring themselves for a dress in January. Fitting that Peter, still trying to live down the fastest title defense collapse in league history, can't even nail a Zoom draft anymore.

Uncut Cokerr Does It Again, Obviously

This is Adam being Adam — 34 and 22 for a reason, the guy who's never had a losing season and just keeps building rosters like the reigning champ he actually is.

The Aura Farm Grew Everything But Quarterback

Christian did the thing good drafters do: he let WR and RB come to him and refused to pay retail at quarterback, and it worked beautifully everywhere except quarterback. CeeDee Lamb at 11 is just clean value, then he backed it up with DeVonta Smith four rounds later at a plus-4 discount and stole Jameson Williams as a luxury WR4. Kittle at 86 is the headline heist of the entire draft, 20 slots below market for a top-five tight end. The RB room of Chase Brown, Etienne, and Tuten is legitimately deep, and Khalil Shakir for nothing in round 11 is the kind of pick that wins bye weeks. Deebo at 107 is the one swing that missed, paying 17 spots over market for a declining role player nobody else wanted.

Then there's the QB room: Trevor Lawrence and Jordan Love, a plus-value tandem on paper that still projects as replacement-level quarterbacking every single week. That's the tax on stacking elite skill talent, and it's very on-brand for the Aura Farm — spectacular ceiling, structurally boom-or-bust, exactly the profile that's gone 10-4 and 4-10 in back-to-back years. This roster can win a title or miss the playoffs from the quarterback position alone.

Rezac Finally Drafts Like He Means It

The running backs are the story here, and it's a good one: Saquon Barkley at 15 was already fair value, but grabbing Jeremiyah Love thirteen spots ahead of market in round 3 gives Logan a legitimate two-headed monster with upside neither of his previous title-curse rosters ever had. Add Dobbins and Allgeier as lottery tickets in the double-digit rounds and the RB room alone justifies the A. Justin Jefferson at 10 is just correct drafting, and letting Jalen Hurts fall to round 6 before scooping him nineteen picks late is the kind of patience that usually isn't in Logan's game. The WR room is fine, not great — McMillan and Burden are bets on unproven volume, and by the time you're relying on Pittman and Worthy for weekly points you're hoping health breaks your way.

Tight end is a dead zone, Isaiah Likely in round 9 a shrug more than a plan, but that's a punt, not a mistake. The real story is whether Logan can finally do something with a good roster in the playoffs. Two straight years he took the 1 seed into the postseason and left with nothing, then wore the dress for good measure in 2025. This team is built to get him back to that stage. What he does there is, as always, the actual question.

This is the same guy who's posted one winning season since 2022, so "boom-or-bust with a great WR corps and a shaky backfield" tracks with the CJ experience. If Burrow plays like a top-8 QB again and the RB dice land right, this is a playoff team. If not, it's another year below the line, just with better receivers to show for it.
