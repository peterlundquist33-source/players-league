2026 DRAFT GRADES

Roster quality + draft-value efficiency vs a 5-source consensus (ESPN ADP, ESPN, FantasyPros ECR, FantasyCalc, Sleeper) · curved · 2026-08-29

Three A's at the top and the draft still felt top-heavy walking out of that cabin — Adam somehow left with the best team despite already owning the best record in league history, which feels unfair on a cellular level, while Peter and John both restocked hard enough to make you forget 2023 ever happened. Running backs went in bunches early and often, and by the time the dust settled half the league had built around two or three backs while the other half punted the position entirely and is going to be very mad about it in Week 6. The middle class is fine — Noah, Christian, Logan all playable — but the bottom five is a genuine disaster, and Kaleb somehow followed up a title with an F, which is either poetic or just sad depending on how much you like Kaleb.

Loaded at WR, quietly stole two QBs

The receiver room is the story here. Rome Odunze in round 5 cost more than his rank suggested, but with JSN and Olave anchoring, that's a luxury reach, not a mistake. The RB room is where this draft gets ugly by design or accident — Kenneth Walker at 18 came in above market, Bucky Irving cost a fourth-round reach, and by the time you're trusting Rico Dowdle and Kenny Gainwell as your depth, you're praying for volume more than talent. That's the D grade at running back, and it's earned. Sam LaPorta anchors a thin TE room, with Mark Andrews as pure round-11 gravy.

The real heist is at quarterback: Jayden Daniels in round 7 and Brock Purdy in round 12 are both absurd on cost alone, Purdy his best value of the whole weekend at plus-40. Brian Robinson in round 13 was dead money from the jump. Adam's the reigning champ chasing his second straight, still trailing Isaac 3-5 head-to-head — this roster says he's not worried about anybody else in the room.

After Bijan, it's Jonathon Brooks, Jordan Mason, Kyle Monangai, and Chris Rodriguez Jr., four straight swings that all missed, Rodriguez the single worst-value pick anyone made this weekend. Tucker Kraft at TE was a reach too. One injury to Bijan and this offense runs through fumes at running back. Fitting for a Lundquist — brilliant instincts, shaky finish, still trailing brother John 3-6 and still chasing that 2022 ceiling.

Tyrone Tracy Jr. was his worst pick, -25, dead weight on a bench that didn't need more bodies at a thin position. John leads Peter 6-3 all-time — he'll need the tight ends to carry the backfield's slack if he wants to keep it that way.

The plan here was obvious from pick one: stack backfield touches and figure out the rest later. Garrett Wilson was a reach at 32, Mike Evans cost seven spots of value he didn't return at WR27, and Josh Downs and Romeo Doubs both went off the board way ahead of their actual production tier. Michael Wilson at #104 (+25) is the one bright spot, a legitimate steal. Ray Davis in round 14 is dead weight nobody drafts on purpose. Vintage Noah — nobody circles him in August, then he's somehow playing in January.

A Reach at Every Turn Adds Up

The quarterback room is the actual instructive part of this draft: Justin Herbert in round 8 and Jared Goff in round 14 were both steals, proof Kaleb can find value when he's patient. This is the 2023 worst-to-first champ now three years into treading water at 8-6, and a draft grade this poor suggests year four of that streak is the good outcome.
